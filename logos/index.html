ChipIn logo options

Decided: option 2

Twelve concepts in three directions. Tell us the numbers you like.

The chip itself

1

Chipped Corner

A square die with its pin-one corner notched out and the missing piece sliding back in: a chip, and the act of chipping in.

2

Open Package Chosen

The chip package drawn as a single-stroke C, open on one side so signals can enter, with the die (the model) held at its centre.

3

One In, One Out

A die stripped to the only two leads it needs, the sensor signal going in on one side and the prediction coming out on the other.

4

Wafer Map

A silicon wafer with its orientation flat, diced into dies, with the centre die lifted out: the one chip that does the job.

The word

5

The open C

The C of ChipIn is an open hand that receives a small square chip, so the first letter already says that the model goes in the chip.

6

The C spirals in

One unbroken trace draws a C and keeps turning inward like an on-chip coil, so the first letter of the name literally goes in.

7

Into the square

A wedge cut drives into a solid square, the simplest picture of something going in, and the wordmark repeats it by turning the light Chip into a heavy In.

8

CI from above

The initials C and I lock together into the outline of a square die seen from above, so the monogram is both the name and the chip.

Signal to decision

9

Many to one

Thousands of candidate models narrow, column by column, to the one chip that ships.

10

Two in, one out

The AND gate the model is compiled into: two signals enter the body and leave as one decision.

11

Signal settles

A noisy sensor wave calms and resolves into a single square, the answer on the chip.

12

Smaller, then smaller

Squares nest into one corner, each smaller than the last, down to the smallest chip that does the job.
