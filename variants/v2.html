Sensors in.

A medical sensing device is three things: sensors, a housing, and one small chip at its core. The sensors pick up a signal from the body and pass it to the chip.

The chip takes that signal and does one job.

Gates.

The job is done by logic gates, wired in place. AI scientists keep redrawing the layout, looking for the smallest circuit that still does the job.

How many logic gates does a diagnosis need?

Taken apart.

The trained model is compiled into the gate layer, with its weights wired in. Then the circuit is checked bit for bit against the model.

Answer out.

Only the answer leaves the chip. It is small, uses little power and works offline.

There is no software on it that an update could change to send raw data out. The patient's signal never leaves the device.

Then the model is wired in.

AI is used to find the circuit. The chip holds a small fine-tuned model wired into its logic gates.

Describe the device. Get back the chip.

An environment, not a chip company. You write a prompt naming the device and its job, and you get back the smallest chip that does it.

  • Small and low power. A wired circuit, no processor running code.
  • Works offline. Nothing to connect to, nothing to send.
  • Outputs only the answer. The raw signal stays on the device.

How a run works

Seven stages, from your prompt to a circuit you can trust. You can watch every one, pause the run and redirect it.

  1. 1SpecYour prompt becomes a precise job.
  2. 2ReferenceA reference model sets the bar.
  3. 3ProposeAI agents propose chip designs.
  4. 4Train (batch)Thousands of candidates train as one GPU batch.
  5. 5CompileThe best become logic gates, weights wired in.
  6. 6VerifyThe circuit is checked bit for bit against the model.
  7. 7ReportYou get the smallest chip that does the job.

An early feasibility test

On a laptop, on Saturday 21 November 2026, one small heart-rhythm detector went through the whole loop.

Accuracy is not yet at clinical level. This test shows the loop works end to end, nothing more.
