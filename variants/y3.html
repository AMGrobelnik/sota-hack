  1. One floor, a few rooms lit.

    Late in the evening, most rooms are dark. One is monitoring brainwaves through the night.

  2. Brainwave monitoring.

    A patient rests in a soft electrode cap. The screen shows sixteen channels and a seizure watch.

  3. The recorder at the bedside.

    It reads the electrodes and decides, on its own chip, whether a seizure is starting.

  4. Inside it, one chip.

    Electrode signals flow in. A prediction flows out to the screen.

  5. A small fine-tuned model.

    It runs on the chip itself and flags a seizure as it starts.

  6. Wired into logic gates.

    The model is compiled into gates with its weights wired in. Each gate is a handful of transistors.

  7. One chip per device.

    Every device gets its own chip, sized for its job. Click a chip to see what it must detect.

What the AI scientists do

  • Propose chip designs for the job
  • Fine-tune thousands of small candidate models at once
  • Compile the best into logic gates, weights wired in
  • Check each circuit against its model
