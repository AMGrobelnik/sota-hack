What will you build?

Describe a medical device and what it must detect. AI scientists design the smallest chip that does it.

Try now Scroll to go inside, one window at a time

  1. One room.

    Night on the ward. A patient sleeps, and the monitor beside the bed keeps watch.

  2. One device.

    It reads heart, oxygen and breathing from the sensors, and decides when to warn.

  3. Look inside.

    Under the casing: the board, a battery, a power supply. And one small chip that makes the call.

  4. The chip.

    Signals from the sensors flow in. A prediction flows out to the screen.

  5. A small fine-tuned model.

    Inside the chip runs a small AI model, fine-tuned for this one job.

  6. Wired into logic gates.

    The model is compiled into logic gates, with its weights wired in. Each gate is a handful of transistors.

  7. Every room, its own chip.

    Each lit window holds a device with a different job. Each needs a chip of its own.

An AI scientist environment.

You say what the device must detect. AI scientists design, train and check the candidates, and hand back the smallest chip that does the job.

  1. What you type

    A device, the signal it reads, what it must catch, and the data to learn from: public, your own, or simulated with stated assumptions.

  2. What the scientists do

    1. Propose many chip designs
    2. Fine-tune many small models, trained together as one GPU batch
    3. Compile the best into logic gates
    4. Check each circuit against its model
    Illustration. Each dot is one candidate design.

  3. What comes back

    Smaller, then smaller. The smallest chip that does the job: its logic gates, the model's weights wired in, and a check of the circuit against the model.

The loop.

One search, run by AI scientists, from a plain description to a finished chip.

Where the speed comes from. Every candidate model in a round is trained together, in one GPU batch, not one after another.
